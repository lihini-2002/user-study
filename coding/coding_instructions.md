# Task-need coding: instructions for a coder

Time needed: about 10 minutes. Please do this on your own, without discussing it with the other coder and without looking at the acceptance results.

## What you are judging

In the study, the assistant flagged pieces of information in what participants typed and suggested replacing them. For each of the 17 rows in the sheet you decide one thing:

**Did the task, as worded in the participant instructions, need this piece of information?**

- **Y (needed)**: the task cannot be completed as intended if this information is replaced or removed. Example of the reasoning, from outside the study: if a task is "book a table for four at 7 pm", the time is needed, and the home address of the person booking is not.
- **N (incidental)**: the task can still be completed as intended without it.

You are not judging the wording of any suggestion, whether the suggestion was good, or what participants did. Judge only the task and the type of information.

## How to fill the sheet

Open `coding_sheet_blank.csv`, save a copy with your initials in the file name, and fill three columns:

1. `needed`: Y or N. Every row needs one of the two.
2. `unsure`: put an x if you think a reasonable person could argue the opposite.
3. `note`: optional, one line of reasoning.

Read the full task wording in the survey instrument (`materials/survey_instrument.md`) before you start; the sheet only carries a one-line summary of each task.

## What happens next

The two filled sheets are compared. We report the percentage of rows on which the two coders agree and Cohen's kappa, resolve disagreements by discussion, and use the agreed codes in the analysis. Rows that stay disputed are analysed both ways.
